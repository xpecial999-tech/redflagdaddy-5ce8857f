## 1. Founder introduction

**Suggested use:** FetLife writing, founder thread, About-page excerpt.

**Opening:** Better conversations before a dynamic gets complicated.

I’m building RedFlagDaddy because important conversations about boundaries,
compatibility and safety are easy to postpone—and surprisingly hard to structure
once feelings, assumptions and excitement are already in the room.

RedFlagDaddy gives two adults private, role-aware prompts to answer separately.
The result is not a verdict on either person. It is a set of scores and
conversation topics designed to make the next direct conversation clearer.

It cannot verify identity, run a background check, diagnose a relationship,
prove consent, respond to an emergency or guarantee anyone’s safety. Consent is
specific, current and revocable; no report replaces paying attention and talking
to each other.

I’m sharing the work gradually and listening before promoting it widely. What
makes a pre-dynamic conversation feel genuinely useful to you rather than
clinical or performative? Please keep replies general—no names, screenshots or
private stories.

**CTA after launch approval:** Create an account at
`https://redflagdaddy.com/`.

## 2. Five-question check-in

**Suggested use:** Text post or five-slide carousel.

**Opening:** Five questions worth answering before “we’ll figure it out” becomes
the plan.

1. What is a clear yes today—and what is definitely not?
2. How can either person pause or stop if words are difficult in the moment?
3. What information stays private, and what may be shared with whom?
4. What kind of check-in or aftercare may be useful afterwards?
5. What change would mean the plan needs to be discussed again?

The aim is not perfect answers. It is making assumptions visible while everyone
can still ask questions without pressure.

**Community question:** Which question would you add?

**Safety footer:** A safeword or checklist does not replace ongoing consent,
attention or an agreed response when someone cannot communicate.

## 3. Hard, soft, curious—and not discussed

**Suggested use:** Educational carousel or short thread.

**Opening:** “We never said no” is not the same as “we agreed.”

- **Hard limit:** not open for negotiation in this context.
- **Soft limit:** may be conditional, uncertain or something to approach with
  extra care.
- **Curious:** worth discussing; not permission to proceed.
- **Not discussed:** no shared understanding exists yet.

The last category matters. Silence, an old assumption or a role label does not
fill the gap. If circumstances, capacity or feelings change, yesterday’s answer
may also be stale.

**Community question:** What language helps you keep “curious” separate from
“yes”?

## 4. When a safeword cannot be used

**Suggested use:** Safety education post.

**Opening:** A safeword is one tool. It is not the entire safety plan.

Before a scene or intense conversation, consider:

- a non-verbal pause or stop signal;
- signs that should trigger an automatic check-in;
- what happens if someone freezes, dissociates, goes quiet or cannot respond;
- who is responsible for noticing changes;
- what “stop now” looks like in practice;
- what support or space each person may want afterwards.

Consent still has to be voluntary, informed, specific and ongoing. A safeword
does not make every other signal irrelevant.

**Community question:** Without sharing a personal incident, what non-verbal
check-in practice do you think more people should discuss?

## 5. Privacy and product expectations

**Suggested use:** Launch-week privacy post or product FAQ.

**Opening:** Private by design still requires careful choices by the people using
the tool.

RedFlagDaddy is designed around private journeys and deliberately enabled
sharing. It does not need a public profile or a social feed, and selective files
can omit scores, overall notes, journey titles and role labels.

But a private link is still a key. A downloaded report is still a sensitive
file. Treat both like something you would not want forwarded, shown on a lock
screen or left in a shared downloads folder.

RedFlagDaddy also cannot verify who typed an answer or prove that consent exists
outside the app. The report is a prompt for a direct conversation, not evidence
that replaces one.

**CTA after launch approval:** Read the consent and safety guide before creating
your first journey.

## 7. Privacy-first export feature

**Suggested use:** Product feature post.

**Opening:** Share less, on purpose.

RedFlagDaddy’s private result tools are designed for different needs:

- complete Markdown or JSON for the owner’s private archive;
- topics-only Markdown without scores or readiness;
- selected dimensions with nothing included until the owner chooses it;
- a conversation plan built from open topics;
- a discreet calendar file with a neutral title and optional, individually
  selected discussion topics.

Selective exports use generic filenames and omit the journey title because a
title can identify someone too. Files are generated on the device rather than
uploaded to create the download.

**Privacy footer:** Review every file before sharing it, and remember that the
recipient controls any copy they receive.
